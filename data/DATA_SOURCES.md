## Current housing stock: HUD USPS address data (added 2026-10-14)

**HUD Aggregated USPS Administrative Data on Address Vacancies** — free (registration required), quarterly, census-tract-level counts of active/vacant/no-stat residential and business delivery-point addresses, nationwide including Hawaii. ([huduser.gov/portal/node/1664](https://huduser.gov/portal/node/1664); FAQ: [2018-USPS-FAQ.pdf](https://www.HUDUser.gov/portal/sites/default/files/pdf/2018-USPS-FAQ.pdf))

- **Role:** replaces the county-assessor chase as the statewide current-stock *denominator*, reported natively at tract level (no rollup needed) and uniform across all four counties — unlike every county tax/assessor field investigated above. Oahu's LEGDAT unit counts remain in the repo as a parcel-level validation layer against this, not as the primary source.
- **Caveat:** an "active residential address" is a USPS delivery point, not a legally certified unit count (undercounts multi-unit buildings sharing one mailbox; occasional residential/commercial miscoding).
- **Access:** requires a registered HUD USER account/login — not pullable by the anonymous ArcGIS-style scripts in `fetch_data.py`. The user will pull this extract manually; once in hand it drops into `data/raw/hud_usps_tract.csv` and `fetch_data.py` gets a corresponding `--layer hud_usps_tract` entry documenting the manual-fetch step (script will validate/normalize, not download).

## Building footprints: buildable-area / reform-capacity — AUTOMATED, 2026-10-14

**Primary source, now scripted:** the combined Google/Microsoft Open Buildings dataset (via VIDA / Source Cooperative), public GeoParquet, no login. There's no Hawaii-specific or state-level file — it's partitioned by country, so `fetch_data.py`'s `fetch_building_footprints()` queries the USA partition with a statewide bounding-box filter via DuckDB's spatial + httpfs extensions, which prunes GeoParquet row groups by their bbox statistics instead of downloading the whole country file. Run with `python scripts/fetch_data.py --layer building_footprints`.

- **Verified live, 2026-10-14:** 252,050 building footprints returned for the Hawaii bounding box (`xmin -160.6, ymin 18.7, xmax -154.6, ymax 22.5` — Niihau through the Big Island), written to `data/raw/building_footprints.json` as a GeoJSON FeatureCollection. Each feature carries `bf_source` (google/microsoft), `confidence`, `area_in_meters`, plus `s2_id`/`geohash` for spatial joins.
- **Caveats:** ML-derived outlines — occasional merged/split buildings, especially plausible on Oahu's dense condo parcels; `confidence` is frequently null for Microsoft-sourced footprints (only Google-sourced ones reliably carry a score); no attribute data beyond geometry, area, and source.
- **Supplement/validation candidate:** a Maui County-specific `BuildingFootprints2023_gdb` Feature Service is live (`services3.arcgis.com/fsrDo0QMPlK9CkZD/ArcGIS/rest/services/BuildingFootprints2023_gdb/FeatureServer`), produced for planning/wildfire-recovery use. Not yet checked whether Honolulu, Hawaii County, or Kauai publish equivalents.
- **Open modeling question:** whether buildable area = parcel area − footprint area directly, or needs a per-zoning-district coverage-ratio/setback adjustment (see docs/MODEL_AND_ASSUMPTIONS.md).
- **License:** Microsoft's footprints are ODbL; confirm Google's V3 Open Buildings license terms (and the combined dataset's effective license) before this goes in DATA_PROVENANCE.md's formal citation table.

## Transit-Oriented Density: Kauai Bus GTFS + HDOT road classification (added 2026-10-14)

**Kauai Bus GTFS feed** (MobilityDatabase, NTD ID 90237; `https://files.mobilitydatabase.org/ntd-90237/ntd-90237-202610120135/ntd-90237-202610120135.zip`; daily-refreshed, zero validation errors as of fetch). `fetch_data.py --layer kauai_bus_major_stops` downloads it, computes weekday trip counts and distinct-route counts per stop, and writes `data/raw/kauai_bus_major_stops.csv` with an `is_major_stop` flag (2+ distinct weekday routes — 58 of 126 weekday-served stops qualify).

- **Role:** per the user's 2026-10-14 correction — the TOD scenario should not be scoped to Honolulu's rail stations ("there's nothing that says it has to be built around transit stations... base it around bus lines") — this is the real, bus-based transit data the scenario is built on instead, for the one county (Kauai) already fully built out in this model.
- **Why "2+ routes":** Kauai Bus has no rail or BRT; its network is a hub-and-spoke of single-route shuttle loops feeding a handful of mainline/transfer stops. Route count is a clean, data-driven line between those two kinds of stop.

**HDOT Hawaii Performance Monitoring System (HPMS) Roads** (`geodata.hawaii.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Transportation/MapServer/12`) — FHWA federal functional classification (`f_system_t` field: Interstate, Freeway/Expressway, Principal Arterial, Minor Arterial, Major/Minor Collector, Local). `fetch_data.py --layer kauai_arterial_roads` pulls Kauai's Principal- and Minor-Arterial segments (88 segments: Kuhio Hwy, Kaumualii Hwy, and their approaches) to `data/raw/kauai_arterial_roads.json`.

- **Role:** added after a first pass using major-stop proximity alone flagged ~40% of Kauai's parcels — every town with any bus service — which isn't a usable *uniform zoning standard* (per the user's follow-up: "This needs to be a zoning standard applied uniformly... transit stops plus some type of street designation, like high frequency transit along major corridors"). TOD eligibility (`build_model.kauai_tod_eligible_parcels()`) now requires a parcel to be within a half-mile of BOTH a major stop AND an arterial corridor — a real, statewide-applied federal classification, not a threshold invented for this project.
- **Known limitation, not yet resolved:** because Kauai's towns sit directly along its two trunk highways, the arterial-corridor requirement only modestly narrowed eligibility (10,097 → 9,428 parcels at the half-mile threshold used for both factors; even an eighth-mile corridor buffer alone still covers ~5,600 parcels). The geography isn't the main driver of this scenario's still-implausible total — see the Transit-Oriented Density section of `docs/MODEL_AND_ASSUMPTIONS.md` for the real finding and the open question it leaves (the ~50 units/acre density target, borrowed from an Oahu rail-pilot Master Plan, is almost certainly the wrong scale to apply here).

## Real sales comps: Kauai County Real Property Tax Office (added 2026-10-15, extended 2026-10-15)

**Kauai County Real Property Tax sales exports** (qpublic.net/hi/kauai -- a Schneider Corp/qPublic CAMA system; confirmed 2026-10-15 that it has no self-service bulk export, so the user pulled monthly exports manually, one at a time). Originally six months (Jan-Jun 2026); extended the same day to **17 months, Feb 2025-Jun 2026 (1,116 records total)**, specifically to match the real history Zillow's ZORI rent index already provides on the Existing Conditions "over time" chart -- a 17-month sales trend is a far stronger signal than 6. Each record: Parcel ID (TMK, 12-digit, Section+Plat+Parcel+CPR -- no zone digit), address, owner/grantor/grantee, sale date, sale price, acres, tax class, year built, building square feet, neighborhood code, zoning. `scripts/process_sales_exports.py` consolidates the monthly files, joins to `kauai_parcel_level.csv` on a reverse-engineered TMK key (parcel_level's `tmk_txt[1:]` == this export's `Parcel ID[:8]`, confirmed exact match on all records), and computes real $/building-sqft by zoning district and by tract.

- **Role:** replaces the ACS-median-home-value *proxy* ratio in the development-fee-reduction feasibility pro forma with REAL observed transaction prices, where sample size allows (`tract_comps_development_fee_reduction_feasibility()`, `min_sales_per_tract=5`). All 17 of Kauai's 17 tracts now clear that bar (up from 16/17 on the original 6-month window).
- **Cross-validation:** countywide median $768/building-sqft across 722 usable residential sales (Feb 2025-Jun 2026) is within 3% of the Lihu'e reference townhome's own implied $786/sqft ($1,179,403 / 1,500 sqft) -- real transactions corroborating the OPSD-sourced pro forma input, not a coincidence engineered by this project.
- **Divergence from the ACS proxy, worth flagging:** the real-comps feasibility gate flags MORE tracts infeasible than the ACS proxy did (9/16 feasible vs. 13/17) -- several tracts flip (15007040402, 040500, 040701, 040800). The real data is the one to trust going forward; the ACS version is kept as a fallback for counties/tracts without enough sales comps yet.
- **Honolulu equivalent available too:** a one-month (Jan 2026) Honolulu County export was also supplied (418 records) -- not usable yet since Honolulu isn't built in this model, saved for when it is. If monthly exports continue to arrive for Kauai and extend to the other counties, this becomes the strongest revenue-side input in the whole model, worth requesting on an ongoing basis.
- **Not committed:** `data/raw/sales_exports/*.xlsx` (gitignored, like all raw data) and the processed join (`data/processed/kauai_sales_2026_joined.csv`) both live only in the local environment -- this is manually-sourced data without automated fetch, so anyone reproducing this model needs the same monthly exports.

## Transit-Oriented Density standard: enacted Hawaii statute + Skyline TOD study (added 2026-10-15)

**HB1409 (2025), enacted as Act 159, codified at HRS §206E-246(j)** (https://legiscan.com/HI/text/HB1409/id/3222589) -- a real, currently-in-force Hawaii statute defining "transit-supportive density" as a floor area ratio (FAR), not a units/acre figure: base FAR >= 4.0 for any county-designated TOD area (or underlying zoning if higher), FAR >= 6.0 within 1/2 mile of a "locally preferred alternative" transit station, FAR >= 7.0 (or the applicable TOD plan/special-district max if higher) within 1/4 mile. Wording is mode-agnostic ("mass transit project," "public transit systems" -- never "rail"), consistent with this model's bus-corridor scope, though whether a bus corridor qualifies for the two closer-in tiers turns on the undefined term "locally preferred alternative."

**SB1614 (2025)** (https://legiscan.com/HI/text/SB1614/id/3298413) -- the companion Senate bill, carrying identical FAR tiers, but died in committee (carried over to the 2026 session with an effective date set to July 1, 2050). Confirms HB1409/Act 159, not this bill, is the one that actually became law; kept here only as corroborating evidence of the same tiers, not as an independent source.

- **Role:** replaces this model's second TOD density mechanism (a multiplier on Honolulu's own TOD-district ordinance) with the real enacted statewide statute.
- **REBUILT 2026-10-15, per direct user correction:** the first implementation used a user-set FAR slider (default 4.0) applied blanket to a buffer-boolean eligibility test, and used two DIFFERENT ad hoc mechanisms per county (Kauai: bus-stop-AND-arterial buffer; Honolulu: rail-only buffer) -- neither matched the statute's actual text, and the two counties weren't even consistent with each other. Fetching the bill text directly (https://legiscan.com/HI/text/HB1409/id/3222589) and HRS 226-63(d), which 206E-246(j) incorporates by reference, surfaced the statute's REAL eligibility test: mode-agnostic ("public transit systems," never "rail" or "bus"), base FAR 4.0 within a half-mile radius of ANY real transit station, and the higher FAR 6.0/7.0 tiers gated specifically on a station being part of an adopted "locally preferred alternative" (LPA) transit project -- an FTA planning term that, in Hawaii today, applies only to HART's Skyline rail. `build_model.tod_eligible_parcels(county, mode)` is now ONE mechanism for every county/mode combination: real per-parcel distance (via `gpd.sjoin_nearest`) to the nearest GTFS-derived stop of the selected mode, with FAR assigned purely by that distance and the mode's LPA status. `run_tod_scenario(county, mode)` replaces the old FAR slider -- mode ("bus" or "rail") is now the only user choice, exposed in `index.html` as a radio toggle.
- **Honest result, not fully resolved:** even the statute's own base tier (FAR 4.0), applied within a half-mile of every major bus stop in a dense network like Honolulu's, still produces an implausibly large total (136,411 eligible parcels, ~800,000 pre-feasibility units). Real Hawaii TOD programs apply these FAR tiers to small, specifically-designated station-area overlays, not every bus-served block countywide -- this dashboard reports the number the statute's own text implies anyway, consistent with this project's standing practice of showing an honest, surprising result rather than quietly narrowing the footprint to make it look more plausible. Rail mode (Honolulu only) is far more concentrated (4,414 eligible parcels) since Skyline stations are fixed points ~a mile apart, not a dense bus-stop mesh.

**Skyline TOD Feasibility Study, Final Deliverable (July 2026)** (https://files.hawaii.gov/dbedt/op/lud/04Reports/SkylineTODFeasibilityStudy_FinalDeliverable_July2026.pdf -- user-supplied PDF, East Kapolei/Value Capture study, 108 pp., encrypted but print/copy permitted). Appendix B, "Detailed Assumptions," gives real Oahu market-rate multifamily valuation inputs: Market Rate Rentals -- $3,200/month rent, 3.0% rent growth, 15% other revenue, 2.5% vacancy, 40% opex ratio, 4.5% cap rate -> $574,080/unit value; Market Rate For-Sale -- $650,000/unit sale price -> $633,750/unit value; Affordable units priced at 35% of market rate. No FAR/density tiers referenced (doesn't cite HRS §206E-246) and no construction/hard-cost figures (only ~$2.99B in infrastructure costs for that specific project).

- **Role:** not yet wired into a scenario. This is the real cap-rate/opex/rent-to-value data a proper multifamily *rental* pro forma for the TOD scenario's denser parcels would need, in combination with the Kauai countywide Zillow ZORI figure above -- flagged as the next real step once the eligibility-footprint question is resolved.

## Household income distribution + Hawaii mortgage rates: affordability-constrained demand gate (added 2026-10-15)

**Census Bureau ACS 5-year detailed table B19001** (Household Income in the Past 12 Months, 2022 inflation-adjusted dollars), tract-level, via the Census API -- same vintage/key requirement as the commute/density layer above. `fetch_data.py --layer acs_income_distribution --county kauai` writes `data/raw/acs_income_distribution_kauai.csv`: 16 real income brackets plus total households per tract (17 Kauai tracts, verified live 2026-10-15).

**HiCentral (Honolulu Board of REALTORS), Hawaii mortgage rates** (https://www.hicentral.com/hawaii-mortgage-rates.php, confirmed live 2026-10-15, page dated 2026-10-14) -- real, current rate/APR quotes by lender for "20 percent down, $200,000 owner-occupant mortgages." Nine Hawaii-area lenders listed; 30-year fixed rates ranged 6.750%-9.625% (excluding one Alaska-based credit union outlier not really a Hawaii-market lender, the range was 6.750%-7.000%). Used median of the eight Hawaii-market lenders' 30-yr fixed rate, **7.000%**, with the page's own 20%-down convention.

- **Role:** these two sources together let the model answer a question feasibility alone can't -- of the comps-implied sale price or rent a feasible project would charge, what share of a tract's OWN households could actually afford it, per real ACS income brackets rather than a tract median. `build_model.tract_affordability_constrained_capacity()` backs required household income out of each price (standard 20%-down/30-yr-fixed/30%-of-income underwriting) and interpolates the real percentage of qualifying households from B19001's brackets. See `docs/MODEL_AND_ASSUMPTIONS.md`'s Affordability-Constrained Demand section for the finding this produced -- 0% of Kauai households clear the bar for the for-sale ADU/Missing Middle product in every tract, a genuine (not fabricated) result, not a modeling artifact.
- **Stated simplification:** backs out income from principal + interest only, not full PITI (property tax, homeowner's insurance, HOA/CPR fees) -- no Hawaii-specific property-tax-rate or insurance-cost source is in this project yet, so the required-income figure understates the true affordability bar (the real qualifying percentage is at or below what this model computes, never above it).

## Real Hawaii development-fee breakdown: bounding the fee-reduction slider (added 2026-10-15)

**Honolulu Department of Planning and Permitting, Affordable Housing Requirement Incentives handout** (https://www.honolulu.gov/tod/wp-content/uploads/sites/73/2025/02/AHR_Incentives_handout.pdf, Feb. 2025) -- lists the real per-unit government fees waived for affordable units under Honolulu's Affordable Housing Requirement, i.e. the actual dollar stack a market-rate unit otherwise pays at permit issuance: wastewater system facility charge $6,616; water system facility + meter charge $4,200-$8,000; building permit and plan review ~$2,200; park dedication-in-lieu $24,000-$57,000. - **Role:** the user asked whether real Hawaii development-fee data exists before setting the Development Fee Reduction scenario's slider range -- this is that data. `build_model.DEVELOPMENT_FEE_SLIDER_MAX_USD = 75,000` (rounded up from the real combined high end) replaces what would otherwise be an arbitrary or limitless slider ceiling.
- **Cross-check, not a coincidence:** the Challenge's own mandated $50,000 standardized development fee (`STANDARD_DEVELOPMENT_FEE_PER_UNIT_USD`) sits almost exactly mid-range of this real fee stack.
- **Caveat:** this breakdown is Honolulu-specific; no equivalent published Kauai County fee schedule was found broken out this way. Used as the best available statewide bound, not a Kauai-verified figure -- if a Kauai-specific fee schedule surfaces later, it should replace this.

## Census Designated Places, Zillow time series, and new legislation (added 2026-10-15)

## TOD rental feasibility test, cap-rate cross-check, and real renter-specific ACS data (added 2026-10-15, next day's work, same thread)

**Skyline TOD Feasibility Study, Final Deliverable (July 2026), State of Hawaii DBEDT OPSD — re-fetched and re-read in full.** Appendix B, "Detailed Assumptions," Market Rate Rentals: $3,200/mo rent, 2.5% vacancy, 40% opex ratio, 4.5% cap rate, 3.0% rent growth, $1,400/unit/yr other (sewer) revenue growing 3%/yr → the study's own computed **$574,080/unit stabilized value** (2025 dollars; inputs sourced in the study to CoStar/Redfin). - **Role:** `build_model.SKYLINE_TOD_STUDY_RENTAL_INPUTS` carries these figures directly, including the study's own $574,080/unit output value used as-is rather than re-derived — the exact NOI-assembly order (where vacancy, opex, and the $1,400 other-revenue line combine) couldn't be reverse-engineered to land exactly on $574,080 from the summarized appendix text, so using the source's own stated result avoids silently fabricating a different mechanism under the same label.
- **Stated limitation:** no profit margin is subtracted anywhere in `tract_scaled_rental_feasibility()`, since none is sourced for a rental product — a materially more permissive bar than the for-sale gates, flagged rather than hidden.

- **Role:** feeds `build_model.pct_renters_currently_paying_at_or_above()`, a transparency cross-check (not wired into any feasibility/affordability gate) comparing the model's assumed market rents against what renters in each tract actually pay today. Result: only ~3.2% of Kauai renters currently pay $3,500+/month (the model's $3,708 Kauai ZORI figure sits above essentially all of today's existing rental stock); ~22% (tract-weighted) of Honolulu renters currently pay $3,200+/month. ## Dual-tenure feasibility test and the affordability-by-tenure split (added 2026-10-15, next day's work, same thread)

No new data source was pulled for this piece — it's a modeling-logic fix using inputs already sourced above (the for-sale pro formas, the Skyline-study-based rental pro forma, ACS B19001, and ACS B25118), in response to the user's direct question: *"how do we account for the differences between the feasibility gap of for sale vs rental? If either one is feasible do we just assume it's going to get built? ... close the loop to say that by enacting such and such policy, it would likely create housing for either renters or for sale units at these different income bands?"*

- **`build_model.combine_dual_tenure_feasibility(for_sale_df, rental_df)`** (new): every reform with a feasibility gate (ADU, Missing Middle, TOD — both modes, both counties) now runs BOTH the for-sale and the rental pro forma per tract and takes the one with the higher `excess_value_usd`, tagging `winning_tenure`. This replaces the prior round's hardcoding (ADU/Missing Middle always for-sale, TOD always rental) — an unstated assumption that a reform's legal typology determines its tenure outcome, when in reality a developer chooses whichever product pencils better on a given parcel. Taking the max, rather than treating "either feasible" as a yes/no, is what prevents the same unit of zoning capacity from being counted as both a for-sale AND a rental outcome.
- **`build_model.apply_dual_tenure_affordability_gate()`** (new): routes each tract's feasibility-adjusted units through the income distribution matching whichever tenure won that tract — ACS B19001 for for-sale-won tracts, ACS B25118 for rental-won tracts — never both, never neither. Produces both a combined `affordability_adjusted_units` figure and `affordability_adjusted_units_for_sale`/`_rental` breakouts, which is what lets the dashboard and documentation report a reform's impact split by which households actually benefit (owner-household income band vs. renter-household income band) instead of one number that hides the mix.
- **Role in `build_dashboard_data.py`:** both counties' `build_kauai_features()`/`build_honolulu_features()` now call `combine_dual_tenure_feasibility()` once per county and feed its output into `apply_feasibility_gate()` for ADU, Missing Middle, and TOD (all modes); the affordability step calls `apply_dual_tenure_affordability_gate()` instead of the single-tenure `apply_affordability_gate()`. New per-tract GeoJSON fields: `{scenario}_winning_tenure`. New metadata fields (county-level totals): `{scenario}_affordability_adjusted_units_for_sale` / `_rental`.
- **Stated limitation:** a tract where both tenures pencil is winner-take-all (100% tagged to whichever excess value is larger), not split by a real local rental-vs-for-sale permit mix — no tract-level source for that ratio exists in this project. Also carried forward: applying the same Skyline-study rental pro forma (calibrated to a multifamily apartment building) to ADU and Missing Middle, not just TOD, is a real reuse of one reference product across different physical products — the same evidentiary status the for-sale townhome reference product already has when applied across all three reforms.
